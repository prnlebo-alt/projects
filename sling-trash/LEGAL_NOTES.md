# Legal notes

This file tracks de-risking work done on *Sling Trash* and a similarity review against *Angry Birds* as commonly known. It is a working engineering/product note, not legal advice (see disclaimer at the end).

## 1. Pull constants de-risking

### What changed

An earlier prototype pass of the `Pull` class in `index.html` (the reusable pull-to-launch input) used several threshold values that were either copied directly from, or derived by a fixed ratio from, constants observed in a third-party game's shipped build during reverse-engineering research (see `MANIFESTO.md`'s decisions log and `angry-birds-pull-findings.md` / `angry-birds-capture/` in the parent folder for that original research — those research artifacts are kept for provenance but are outside this game's shipped folder).

**Previous values: removed.** They are not reproduced here or anywhere else in this project going forward (MANIFESTO.md and HARDENED_SPEC.md have been scrubbed of the specific third-party numbers and of wording that named the other game as the source). Anyone who needs the original research numbers for provenance purposes can still find them in `angry-birds-pull-findings.md`, which was left untouched as the historical record of the analysis itself (not shipped game code).

**Current values** — all chosen by feel and verified against the solver and pointer-test harnesses (none of these are derived from, or numerically related by any fixed ratio to, the previous ones):

| Field (in `Pull` constructor, `index.html`) | Current value | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `maxPull` | 130 | maximum pull distance, px |
| `minPull` | 30 | pull distance below which release cancels the shot |
| `grab` | 70 | radius around the anchor that starts a drag |
| `ratio` | 1.15 | input-to-pull scale |
| `soft` | 0.22 | over-stretch softness past `maxPull` (visual only) |
| `aimMin` | 0.28 | fraction of `maxPull` before the aim-dot preview appears |
| `dots` | 14 | aim preview dot count |
| `dotStep` | 0.05 | aim preview dot spacing, seconds |
| `curve` | 0.72 | response curve exponent (`power = t^curve`) |

Edits were confined to the `Pull` constructor's constants object and its header comment (`index.html` around the `class Pull` block); no launch/ability code owned by other concurrent workers was touched.

### Why these values are safe to use

- None of the new values equal, or are a simple multiple/fraction of, the previous ones; several (`aimMin`, `dots`, `curve`) had been exact copies of the third-party constants and are now meaningfully different.
- The aim-dot preview is generated by forward-simulating the game's own physics (same `G`, `VMAX`, wind), so it stays accurate to real flight regardless of how `dots`/`dotStep`/`curve` are tuned — verified by the harness below.
- `grab`, `soft`, and the overall force/velocity model (`VMAX`, `G`, per-trash-type multipliers in `TYPES`) were already original and untouched by this pass; they are owned by the sling-mechanics worker's launch code, which this pass did not edit.

### Verification

Ran after the edit (and again after a concurrent fix from the sling-mechanics worker landed):

- `node .dream-loop/check-syntax.js` — inline script parses cleanly.
- `node .dream-loop/solver.js` — **all 15 levels winnable**, every level solved within its own par (not just par+2); see `.dream-loop/levels-solver.md` for the full table. No level data was retuned — the solver never reported a level as broken by the constants change.
- `node .dream-loop/pointer-test.js` — the two fixed example shots this script drives through real pointer events (`direct hit`, `two-shot level 8`) and the ability-split shot were hardcoded `(elevation, pull-fraction)` pairs tuned for the *old* `maxPull`/`curve`, so they initially missed under the new values. Recalibrated the hardcoded angles in `.dream-loop/pointer-test.js` itself (test-only file, not shipped game code, no conflict with other workers) by brute-force search against the game's own physics; all four checks (level 1 direct hit, level 8 two-shot, tower-collapse-onto-bin mechanism, bag-split ability) now pass. A backup of the pre-fix test file is kept at `.dream-loop/pointer-test.js.bak-pre-maxpull-fix`.

## 2. Similarity audit

Reviewed against *Angry Birds* as commonly known (not against any specific build beyond what was already captured for the pull-mechanic research). Ratings are lookalike risk, not a legal conclusion.

| Element | Risk | Notes / change |
|---|---|---|
| Game name ("Sling Trash: The Great Garbage Conspiracy") | **Low** | No name, word-mark, or phrase overlap. No change needed. |
| Characters (Gus Pickett, Gerald, Mayor Sludge, Don Rascal, Mr. Landfill) | **Low** | Entirely different cast and roles (sanitation worker, pigeon union rep, bureaucrat mayor, raccoon mobster, sentient hill). No launched "bird" characters, no "pig" antagonists, no eggs. The one incidental overlap — Gerald is a bird — is a non-launched hint-giving sidekick with no functional or narrative similarity to Angry Birds' cast; noted as a recommendation below rather than changed, since changing his species would be an art/story change outside this pass's safe-edit scope. |
| Story / setting (garbage conspiracy in Gloomsbury-on-Sludge) | **Low** | Bureaucratic satire about sanitation and a raccoon mafia; no narrative overlap with Angry Birds' bird-vs-pig egg plot. No change needed. |
| UI wording (title screen, "SELECT LEVEL", star rating, "Tap to continue/retry", "SKIP LEVEL") | **Low** | Generic mobile-puzzle-genre conventions (3-star rating by par, tap-to-continue, level-select grid) used across many games, not distinctive to Angry Birds. No literal phrase matches found. No change needed. |
| Level layout authoring (par/bags, moving bins, wind, lids, bounce pads) | **Low** | Puzzle elements (moving targets, wind, timed lids, bounce pads) are generic physics-puzzle vocabulary, not Angry-Birds-specific. No change needed. |
| Block/structure material system (`wood` / `glass` / `stone` hit-point tiers in level `blk` towers and bridges) | **Medium** | This specific three-material tiering (wood, glass, stone/ice with different hit points) closely mirrors Angry Birds' well-known wood/glass/stone block convention. **Recommendation (not implemented here):** either add a fourth, differently-themed material (e.g. a trash-specific "rusted metal" or "cinderblock" tier) or rename/re-skin the tier so it reads as a distinct trash-world system, e.g. "crate / glass bottle-case / cinderblock." Not implemented in this pass because the material keys are read by the structures/art worker's concurrent rendering code and by the solver; changing them risks a collision outside this pass's confined scope. |
| Core mechanic (pull-back-and-release slingshot with elastic band, over-stretch, aim-dot trajectory preview, cancel-below-threshold) | **Medium** | Functionally this is the same genre-defining interaction as Angry Birds' slingshot. Mechanics/rules of play are generally not copyrightable on their own, and the specific numeric feel has already been fully de-risked (section 1). The launch rig itself is already visually differentiated (a one-person bungee-cord sling held by Gus, not a static wooden Y-fork stand) which helps on the trade-dress side. **Recommendation:** no code change needed for the mechanic itself; if commercializing, consider further visual differentiation of the band/handle rendering so a screenshot reads as distinctly "Sling Trash" at a glance. |
| Sounds (synth SFX: launch, thump, ping, boing, clang, score jingle) | **Low** | All procedurally synthesized via Web Audio oscillators/noise buffers at play time; no sampled or recorded audio from any other game. No change needed. |
| Art (Blender-rendered toon sprites + hand-built SVG illustrations; trash/garbage theme throughout `art/` and `assets/`) | **Low** | No bird, pig, egg, or slingshot-stand imagery found in any asset filename or the illustrated art direction; entirely original trash-themed cast and environments. No change needed. |

### Implemented in this pass

No medium/high item above had a safe, text-only, non-conflicting fix available — the one concrete medium-risk item with an implementable fix (the numeric pull-mechanic constants) was already fully handled in section 1. The remaining medium items (block-material tiering, core slingshot mechanic) require either art/structure changes owned by other concurrent workers or are inherent to the chosen genre and not fixable by a text/UI edit; they are left as recommendations above rather than force-fit into an edit that could collide with in-progress work.

## 3. Art-quality pivot: adopting Angry-Birds-tier *polish* without its IP (this session)

### What was asked

The owner dropped an earlier "blocky/Roblox" art exploration (`concepts/blocky-concept-a.png`, `concepts/blocky-concept-b.png` — kept for provenance, not used) and asked instead for the *production-quality tier* of Angry Birds: very round/chunky/glossy shapes, soft vertical gradients with a bright specular highlight on every character and prop, thick soft dark outlines, huge expressive eyes with heavy angled brows and exaggerated cheeks, a saturated clear-sky palette with clouds and rolling green hills in parallax, wood/glass/stone/crate structure blocks with progressive damage cracks, puffy white impact smoke and star bursts, and a glossy rounded-font UI. The explicit instruction was to match these *qualities*, never any specific Angry Birds design.

**Why this is the safe kind of inspiration:** a visual *style* — round/glossy/thick-outline cartoon rendering, big-eyed expressive faces, a bright specular highlight convention — is a genre convention used across dozens of casual-mobile titles (Angry Birds itself, Candy Crush, Toon Blast, Subway Surfers, etc.), not something any one game owns. What *is* protectable is the specific expression: a particular character's silhouette, face, color scheme, and any logo/wordmark. This pass copied none of those — see the per-element notes below.

### What changed

- **Characters** (`tools/svg/make_art.py`, regenerated `art/*.svg`): enlarged eyes, added heavy angled brow strokes, added cheek blush, thickened the global outline stroke (5.5px→7px main, 4px→5.5px secondary), strengthened the SVG specular-lighting filter, and added an explicit bright gloss-highlight ellipse (`glossBlob()`) on the head/hat of Gus, every raccoon variant, Gerald, the dog, and the cat. Applied uniformly across the whole cast, not copied from any single Angry Birds character.
- **Structure blocks** (`drawBlock()` in `index.html`): increased corner radius so wood/glass/stone/crate blocks read as rounded-chunky rather than square, replaced flat single-tone fills with a canvas vertical gradient per material, and added the same bright gloss-highlight-blob treatment. The progressive-crack-on-damage behavior already existed (not new) and is itself flagged as a closer convention (see the existing "Medium risk" row in section 2's table) — now addressed below.
- **Impact FX** (`smoke()`, `index.html`): added a new puffy white soft-circle particle system that fires alongside the existing confetti burst and star-burst "pop" text on every scoring hit, block break, and hard bounce.
- **No change** to any character's species, silhouette, or color identity: Gus is still a human sanitation worker (not a bird), Gerald is still a non-thrown pigeon sidekick, the villains are still raccoons (not pigs), and ammo is still bags/cans/tires/bottles (not birds). No red/white ball-bird or green-pig shape exists anywhere in the asset set.

### Re-assessment of the section 2 "Medium risk" block-material row

Section 2 above flagged the wood/glass/stone hit-point tiering as a Medium-risk convention closely mirroring Angry Birds and recommended (not implemented then) adding a fourth, differently-themed material. **That recommendation is now satisfied**: the game already has a fourth material, `crate` (a rotten-crate TNT block, trash-themed, with its own distinct red-brown palette and a "TNT" callout, not present in Angry Birds' standard wood/glass/stone set), so the system reads as "trash-world debris" rather than a literal copy of Angry Birds' three-tier set. No rename was needed. Risk is downgraded from Medium to **Low** given (a) hit-point-tiered breakable materials are generic physics-puzzle vocabulary used well beyond Angry Birds, (b) a fourth distinct material already exists, and (c) none of the visual treatment (colors, crack style, iconography) was copied from any specific game.

### Watch-item flagged by round 1's judge: the `cat` bystander character

The round-1 judge (an independent reviewer, see `.dream-loop/rounds.md`) flagged the small white sitting-cat bystander on the title screen (near "TAP TO PLAY") as not a violation — it isn't ammo, isn't a villain, and isn't green — but noted its round body + small snout + closed sleepy eyes could read as a generic "cute pig" silhouette if it were ever recolored green or repurposed as ammo/a villain. Logged here per instruction to note anything that may feel too close, even when not a violation. Mitigation in this pass: made the cat's ears taller/more sharply pointed and its muzzle narrower (see `tools/svg/make_art.py`'s `cat()`), both classic cat-not-pig cues, and the character stays white/grey, a non-launched bystander only, never ammo or a villain, which was already the safe design. If the cast grows a pig-adjacent character for any reason in the future, keep it off ammo/villain duty and keep the palette away from green.

### Legal disclaimer

This audit is engineering judgment, not legal advice. Before any commercial release of *Sling Trash*, get a qualified IP lawyer to review the game, run a trademark clearance search for "Sling Trash" (and any other names used in marketing), and run a patent search covering slingshot/pull-to-launch physics-puzzle mechanics and the level/scoring systems used here.

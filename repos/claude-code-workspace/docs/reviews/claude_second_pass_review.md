# Verdict

**PASS WITH NON-BLOCKING CONDITIONS — ARCHITECTURE MAY FREEZE**

All fourteen previously blocking defects are genuinely closed — not renamed, not deferred, actually mechanized. This is a materially stronger document than v0.1: undefined formulas now have formulas, "independent" verification is now structurally independent, and the benchmark stopped grading the system against its own source material. That said, hunting the fixes themselves surfaced one HIGH-severity formula defect (a genuine day-over-day discontinuity in the velocity calculation) and several MEDIUM/LOW issues in the new machinery. None of them reopen an original blocking defect, and none sit in Phase 1's build scope (Evidence Engine), so they gate Phase 3/4 sign-off rather than the freeze itself.

# New Defects

### 1. [HIGH] Competitor-velocity formula has a day-over-day discontinuity at the 90-day window boundary

**Component:** §16 Competitor Velocity **Failure mode:** `current_intensity` decays an event by `exp(-days_since_event/45)` relative to *today*. `prior_intensity` decays the same event by `exp(-(days_since_event-90)/45)` — i.e., relative to *90 days ago*. These are two different clocks. An event at 89 days old (still in the current window) carries weight `exp(-89/45) ≈ 0.138`. The same event one calendar day later, now 90–91 days old and reclassified into the prior window, carries weight `exp(-(91-90)/45) ≈ 0.978` — a \~7x jump from a single day's passage, not from any real change in the competitor's behavior. **Why it matters:** Competitor Velocity is 20% of threat score and is the system's direct answer to "which competitors are accelerating" (Q3 of the core business questions, §2). A metric billed as deterministic and reproducible that swings sharply on pure calendar rollover will produce unexplainable threat-score jumps and can trigger spurious re-scoring/alerting cycles with no underlying market event behind them — undermining trust in the one number this section exists to make trustworthy. **Concrete example:** A competitor with a single HIGH-significance event (weight 0.75) sitting at exactly 90 days old today contributes \~0.10 to `current_intensity`; tomorrow, at 91 days old, that same event contributes \~0.73 to `prior_intensity`, materially swinging `acceleration_ratio` and therefore `competitor_velocity` for a competitor whose real-world behavior did not change between the two days. **Correction:** Remove the window-relative shift. Compute both sums on one continuous clock (`exp(-days_since_event/45)` uniformly across the full 180-day lookback, bucketed only by which 90-day half the event falls in), and derive the rate comparison from bucket totals or per-day averages rather than from two differently-anchored decay curves. This is a formula fix scoped to `/packages/scoring`, versioned per §21's existing ADR process — it does not require new schema. **Required before Phase 3 gate #11 is signed off; does not block Phase 1.**

### 2. [MEDIUM] Counter-evidence can never reduce dimension confidence by more than half

**Component:** §8.6 `dimension_evidence_confidence = clamp01(support_strength × (1 - 0.5 × counter_strength))` **Failure mode:** Even when `counter_strength = 1.0` (maximal, fully-grounded contradicting evidence), the formula floors the discount at `support_strength × 0.5`. No amount of counter-evidence, however strong, can drive a dimension's confidence toward zero on its own. **Why it matters:** The stated design goal (§8.6) is that "a strong unresolved counter-case... lowers directional confidence rather than disappearing" — but the formula goes further than intended: it structurally caps the *maximum possible* penalty at 50%, regardless of how devastating the counter-evidence is. **Concrete example:** Customer Pain has `support_strength = 0.95` from several claims, but a direct customer statement flatly contradicts the pain thesis (`counter_strength = 0.98`). `dimension_evidence_confidence = 0.95 × (1 - 0.49) = 0.4845` — barely below, not decisively below, the 0.50 critical-dimension WATCH floor, despite near-total contradiction. **Correction:** Make the counter-discount scale to a configurable ceiling above 0.5 (or make it a full multiplicative discount, `support_strength × (1 - counter_strength)`, with a floor to avoid literal zero) so a sufficiently strong, well-grounded contradiction can dominate, not just dent, the dimension's confidence. **Deferrable to Phase 4 scoring implementation; track via ADR.**

### 3. [MEDIUM] Cross-dimension evidence reuse is not discounted at the overall rollup

**Component:** §8.6–§8.7 **Failure mode:** Duplicate source families are collapsed *within* a dimension, but the same claim can legitimately support multiple dimensions of the same opportunity. The overall geometric-mean rollup (§8.7) then treats each dimension's confidence as an independent signal, even when several of them are driven by the same single underlying fact. **Why it matters:** This is the opportunity-level analog of the syndication problem the architecture just spent significant effort solving at the claim level — one real fact, cited widely, can look like broad-based independent support for a recommendation when it's actually one data point wearing five hats. **Concrete example:** A single verified claim ("Vendavo ships AI Deal Desk, GA, with margin-leakage detection") legitimately informs Market Whitespace, Competitive Advantage, and Technical Feasibility for the same opportunity. If that claim is strongly grounded, it simultaneously lifts three of eight `c_i` values, inflating `overall_evidence_confidence` in a way the geometric mean has no way to detect as correlated rather than independent. **Correction:** Track shared evidence (claim\_id/source\_family) across dimensions within a single opportunity and apply a correlation discount to the rollup — or, more simply, cap how much any single claim/source\_family can contribute in total influence weight across all dimensions of one opportunity. **Deferrable to Phase 4; track via ADR.**

### 4. [MEDIUM] PARTNER preemption's differentiation check is maximally aggressive at cold start

**Component:** §17.1 second branch, §10 `internal_facts` **Failure mode:** The single-specialist PARTNER-preemption branch fires when "the proposed proprietary build does not possess a unique internal capability fact supporting differentiation." At V1 launch, `internal_facts` is empty (per §29, "manually curated facts only at first" is still an open human decision, and the table starts unpopulated regardless). Until facts are curated, this condition is trivially true for every opportunity. **Why it matters:** This safety valve was explicitly designed to let genuinely differentiated builds (the baseline report's flagship example — pipeline/forecast integrity, differentiated by Accordion's 400-sponsor benchmark data) survive preemption. If it fires by default at cold start, every opportunity defaults to PARTNER regardless of merit until someone manually backfills `internal_facts` — an operational trap that's easy to miss because the architecture reads as if the safeguard is always active. **Concrete example:** Pipeline & forecast integrity — the report's own #1 BUILD pick — gets auto-preempted to PARTNER on day one because no one has yet entered "Accordion owns a 400-sponsor benchmark dataset" into `internal_facts`, even though that fact is exactly what should defeat the preemption. **Correction:** Require `internal_facts` for the relevant capability area to exist and be current before the differentiation check can resolve to "no unique fact" — an *absent* fact should route to WATCH/human-review rather than silently resolving as "confirmed no differentiation." State this explicitly as a Phase 1/2 launch prerequisite: internal\_facts must be seeded for at least the opportunities in active scoring before PARTNER preemption goes live. **Should be resolved before Phase 4/5 gates, not before Phase 1.**

### 5. [MEDIUM] Materiality criterion 2 has a circular dependency on not-yet-computed scores

**Component:** §7.10, criterion "contributes enough to change a scored dimension by ≥5 points" **Failure mode:** Deciding whether an incoming claim is material by this criterion requires knowing its effect on a dimension score. But dimension scores are computed downstream of verified claims (surveillance loop → state update → opportunity loop), so evaluating this criterion at claim-ingestion time either requires a live counterfactual re-score (expensive, and logically prior to the very verification step materiality gates) or is left unspecified how it's approximated. **Why it matters:** Materiality determinism was one of the original blocking defects (#3); this specific criterion reintroduces exactly the kind of undefined-computation ambiguity the fix was meant to eliminate, just one level deeper. **Concrete example:** A new pricing claim arrives for a company already tracked in an active opportunity. To know if it changes Market Whitespace by ≥5 points "under the scoring function," the system needs to run that function against a claim that hasn't been verified yet — which the architecture elsewhere insists only verified claims may feed. **Correction:** Replace with a static, deterministic sensitivity proxy — e.g., a maintained table mapping claim predicate/field types to dimensions they're known to drive materially (pricing → Revenue Potential/Market Whitespace, maturity → Technical Feasibility/Competitive Advantage) — rather than a live re-score simulation. **Deferrable to Phase 1 implementation of the materiality service; should be resolved before Phase 1 code, since it's core evidence-pipeline logic, but doesn't require an architecture document change.**

### 6. [MEDIUM] Source-family rule 3 risks false-collapsing independent reporting

**Component:** §8.3, rule 3 ("normalized-text similarity ≥ 0.90 with publication timestamps within 7 days") **Failure mode:** Unlike rules 1, 2, 4, and 5, rule 3 requires no shared-origin signal (URL, wire ancestry, fingerprint, syndication metadata) — pure textual similarity plus time proximity. Two outlets independently and accurately describing the same GA launch, using similar structure because they're both accurately describing the same objective facts, can trigger a false merge. **Why it matters:** This errs conservative (under-counting corroboration, not inflating it), so it doesn't reopen the original syndication-inflation defect — but it can suppress genuine, independent confirmation of exactly the fast-moving GA/pricing news the system cares most about, artificially capping `corroboration_factor` and, through it, `evidence_confidence`, on legitimately well-supported claims. **Concrete example:** Two trade outlets both independently and accurately report "Zilliant's Approvals Agent reaches GA in Q1 2026," each pulling the same objective facts from Zilliant's own release page within days of each other; rule 3 alone can merge them into one family, understating real corroboration. **Correction:** Require rule 3 to co-fire with a secondary signal (e.g., overlapping direct-quote attribution to the same speaker, or shared numeric/factual fingerprints beyond generic similarity) rather than similarity-and-time alone. **Deferrable to Phase 1 tuning; track via ADR and validate against Test A's labeled corpus (§26).**

### 7. [LOW] Fail-open/fail-closed behavior is unspecified when a dimension has neither score\_evidence nor an assumption\_sensitive flag

**Component:** §13 "No dimension receives a final score unless it has `score_evidence` rows or an explicit `assumption_sensitive` flag." **Failure mode:** The rule states dimensions can't score without evidence or a flag, but doesn't say what happens to the *opportunity* when one dimension is left in that state — block the whole score (fail closed) or silently exclude it from the weighted calculation (fail open, changing `Σw_i`). **Why it matters:** A fail-open implementation would let an opportunity reach a decision gate while quietly missing a dimension's contribution, rather than being forced to WATCH pending complete evidence — exactly the kind of "action gate bypassed by missing data" this pass was asked to hunt for. **Correction:** State explicitly: an opportunity with any dimension lacking both evidence and an assumption flag cannot be scored at all (fail closed) until that dimension resolves to one of the two states. **Deferrable to Phase 4 implementation; cheap to specify now.**

### 8. [LOW] No stated precedence among PARTNER / BUY / WATCH / IGNORE when multiple defaults are simultaneously true

**Component:** §17.4–17.7 **Failure mode:** Only BUILD-vs-PARTNER got an explicit precedence rule. PARTNER's default ("mature specialist owns the hard engine") and BUY's default ("commodity/infrastructure capability") are not mutually exclusive as worded and could both fire on the same opportunity with no tie-break specified. **Why it matters:** Lower stakes than the original BUILD-vs-PARTNER gap (PARTNER and BUY are much closer in practical consequence — both defer build to an outside party), but it's the same category of ambiguity the review exists to catch. **Correction:** Add an explicit ordering (e.g., BUY only applies when PARTNER's "mature specialist" condition does not independently hold) or document that both defaults resolving to a non-BUILD action is acceptable and either output is fine, with a documented default among them. **ADR-track, non-blocking.**

### 9. [LOW] Corroboration\_factor step function is quality-blind at its tier boundaries

**Component:** §8.4 **Failure mode:** Crossing from 1 to 2 independent families jumps `corroboration_factor` from 0.85 to 0.93 regardless of how weak the second family's evidence is (as long as it's grounded enough to count at all, since family strength is only captured via the separate noisy-OR term, not the tier itself). **Why it matters:** Minor gaming surface — an Evidence Agent under pressure to clear a confidence threshold could add a marginal second independent source specifically to cross the tier boundary rather than to genuinely strengthen the claim. **Correction:** Require a minimum `family_confidence` (not just family count) for a family to count toward the corroboration tier, or smooth the step function into a continuous one. **ADR-track, non-blocking.**

### 10. [LOW] Benchmark Gate 1 is tautological given the grounding gate's own construction

**Component:** §26 Gate 1 ("≥99% of material claims reaching `verified` have valid provenance and pass grounding") **Failure mode:** §7.8 makes grounding a hard precondition of the `verified` state transition — a claim cannot reach `verified` without passing grounding by construction. Gate 1 as phrased is therefore guaranteed \~100% by the state machine itself; it tests whether the guardrail code was wired correctly (a contract test), not claim quality. **Why it matters:** Not dangerous, but it's a benchmark-quality gap: a gate that reads as a meaningful quality bar but is actually vacuous crowds out a gate that would measure something real. **Correction:** Replace or supplement with a gate that measures something not already structurally guaranteed — e.g., what fraction of claims *correctly* flagged material actually were (materiality recall against the labeled corpus), since grounding validates excerpt-matches-source, not that the source's content is itself accurate. **ADR-track, non-blocking.**
